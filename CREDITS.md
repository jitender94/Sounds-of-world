### Approximate matches — worth replacing

- **taarab**: the clip is Tanzanian *ngoma* drumming, not taarab orchestra.
  Right country, wrong genre.

  kriti performance. Only ~12s before it loops.

### Approximate matches in this batch

- **sufi-ney**: an authentic Turkish 78, but a popular song rather than a
  Mevlevi sema ceremony — no solo ney taksim.
- **qawwali**: a vintage Indian 78 with harmonium and tabla, but not
  confirmed to be qawwali proper.
- **taonga-puoro**: Frances Alda was a Western-trained opera singer performing
  a Māori waiata, so the arrangement is Europeanised.

### Still without their own recording (2)

These still fall back to their cluster's clip and remain **mislabelled** —
fix before publishing:

- `griot-kora` — plays the West African djembe clip. Free kora recordings are
  genuinely scarce.
- `dombyra` — plays the Tuvan throat-singing clip. Archive hits for
  "Turkestan" were American novelty songs, not Kazakh music.

Rejected for licensing along the way: several excellent qawwali, kora and
dombra recordings on the Archive carry no stated licence (community uploads of
commercial releases) and must not ship. A CC BY-NC-ND ney improvisation was
also rejected — the ND term forbids the trimming this site does.

Commons has no freely-licensed recording of these that I could find. Afrobeat
in particular is a problem: the Fela-era repertoire is all in copyright.
Likely better sources: the Internet Archive's live-music collections, the
Smithsonian Folkways free previews, or Free Music Archive.

Notes for curation pass (user to review by ear):
- west-africa: 11s drum loop — loops cleanly but is percussion-only.
- east-africa: begena scale demonstration, not a performance; license not
  confirmed — replace if a better krar/masenqo recording is found.
- central-asia: authentic khoomei technique, but the melody is the
  Internationale — consider replacing with a traditional Tuvan/Kazakh piece.
- oceania: pure didgeridoo drone; hypnotic, fits songline mood.
